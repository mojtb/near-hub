## Why the gaps exist

- **`active_accounts_30d_v1_vs_v3`**: NearBlocks documents neither count, but the numbers show what each one is. v1 matches Nansen's published distinct signers, and v3 stays within 2% of v1 plus meta_accounts, so it also counts users whose transactions a relayer signs. The gap is therefore the size of relayed activity: about 1.8x now, but 9x to 18x in monthly averages before 2026-04-02, when a Kai-Ching batch of about 2,000,000 accounts a day stopped and a Sweat key migration ended. Both counts are kept here, and neither is called daily active users.
- **`burn_before_issuance_model_vs_supply_fall`**: Expected: the burn figure is a lower bound, and this is the one place it can be measured. Before issuance began on 2020-10-20 nothing was minted, so every fall in total supply was gas burned. Over those 79 days supply fell by 124.4 NEAR against gas fees of 146.8 NEAR, which is 84.8% of gas, while the burn figure takes 70%. The contracts' share was 30% of the gas burnt executing function calls, not of all gas, so the real burn was always more than 70% of the fee and never more than all of it. The lower bound held on every one of the 79 days, and a test keeps it so. The 84.8% belongs to 2020's mix of transactions, so it is not applied to later years; treat the burn figure before 2026-11-04 as a floor. From that day protocol version 87 burns all gas and the figure is exact.
- **`circulating_supply_chain_vs_published_schedule`**: The Launch row differs by design. NEAR's post warns that unclaimed tokens, over 300M at the time, sat in unlocked accounts until claimed, so the chain shows more unlocked than the plan until those lockups were made in the following weeks. NearBlocks' figure for that day is within 0.03% of the chain-built one. The later rows stay within 5%: the plan assumes full issuance and no burning, and lockups kept being created after launch.
- **`circulating_supply_latest_nearblocks_vs_coinmetrics`**: Both figures are retired, and they were never independent. NearBlocks computed circulating supply from lockup contracts until its 2025-09-03 rewrite removed that job, so /v1/charts and /v1/stats have read 1,249,836,992 NEAR since 2025-08-27. CoinMetrics takes project-reported supply through CoinGecko, and NEAR's report is NearBlocks' legacy endpoint: the two stayed within 0.02% (median) from November 2024, CoinMetrics carried the frozen figure for two months, and on 2025-10-25, two days after NearBlocks pointed that endpoint at total supply, it jumped to total supply and has followed the previous day's total ever since. Circulating supply is now built from the lockup contracts on chain.
- **`circulating_supply_year_mean_chain_vs_nearblocks`**: Only 2024 differs, and the fault is NearBlocks'. From 2024-08-12 its job counted a lockup it failed to read three times as nothing locked, so RPC failures pushed its figure as high as 99.95% of total supply. Its commit of 2024-11-04 made such a failure stop the job instead, so the figure stood still until 2024-12-28. Outside that window the two are close every day, and a test checks that they stay so.
- **`commits_52w_statistic_vs_default_branch`**: Two definitions, not an error. GitHub's weekly statistic counts commits beyond the default branch: near/nearcore keeps release branches, and a sweep of one week found commits on 2.14-release. The commits list, the search API and GraphQL's history all give the default-branch count. The statistic runs below the default branch for other repositories (ton-blockchain/ton, near/near-api-js) and returns a year of zeros for anza-xyz/agave, so the default-branch count is used for every repository.
- **`etf_nrr_near_chain_vs_fund`**: The chain read covers only NEAR delegated to the fund's main pool, and it includes rewards the fund may still owe as fees. A gap that keeps growing would mean the fund uses wallets or a pool that is not read here.
- **`etf_nrr_near_delivered_chain_vs_fund`**: The chain side is NEAR that moved into or out of the listed wallets after the seed day; the fund side is each day's change in shares times the NEAR behind one share. On the days of 1 to 9 October the split between days was itself taken from the chain, but the total to 12 October comes from reported totals, so the two sums are independent. A gap that grows would mean NEAR arrives or leaves outside the wallets Bitwise lists.
- **`intents_pool_dollars_month_ends_chain_vs_dune`**: Only the month ends before the dashboard carried USDT0 differ, and the fault is the dashboard's. Its series by asset has no value for USDT0 until 2026-03-09, though the chain shows intents.near holding that token at the month ends before. The other dollar tokens agree asset by asset to within one percent on those days too, so the gap is that one token, and the dashboard's dollars run low before that day. From it on the chain still reads a little above the dashboard at most month ends, by about what the pool holds of USDT bridged from Scroll, which sits inside HOT's contract and which the dashboard appears to leave out.
- **`intents_revenue_all_time_defillama_vs_page_monthly`**: Open question. Counted as dollars on the day they arrived (USDC at $1, NEAR at the transfer's value), what the three wallets received is within 2% of the page's monthly series from October 2025 to March 2026. From April 2026 the series runs above the receipts in every month. The receipts themselves are not in doubt: DefiLlama's count of the same wallets matches the chain transfers within 0.3%, and the page's own wallet table matches them within 0.01%. NEAR does not publish how the monthly series is built, and no cause is confirmed. Treat the wallet-based figure as the checkable one; the site's Revenue page draws both.
- **`intents_volume_2026_apr_aug_defillama_vs_page`**: DefiLlama's Intents volume changes meaning on 2026-09-01. From 2026-04-01 to 2026-08-31 it matches NearBlocks' public-only figure within 0.2% (see intents_volume_2026_apr_aug_defillama_vs_nearblocks), and from 2026-09-01 it equals revenue.near.org's all-routes figure, which includes confidential Intents. Its adapter carries no date rule, so the likely cause is that DefiLlama kept its stored daily values when NEAR restated the earlier months; that is not confirmed. So all-route volume is taken from revenue.near.org and public volume from NearBlocks, and DefiLlama's series is used only in this comparison. Its fees change scope the same way, through 2026-09-13.
- **`treasury_share_30d_config_vs_epoch_rewards`**: The RPC config field reads 0, but treasury.near received EpochRewards in every epoch read so far, close to 10% of issuance. Trust the observed flow, not the config field.
- **`tvl_latest_defillama_vs_nearblocks`**: Not explained, because NearBlocks does not document what its TVL figure counts. DefiLlama's figure is what sits in lending markets, DEXs and restaking on NEAR. NearBlocks' was over three quarters of it a year ago and is about a third now, so the two are not one measure with different rules. The site reads DefiLlama's and does not show NearBlocks'.
