## Why the gaps exist

- **`active_accounts_30d_v1_vs_v3`**: NearBlocks documents neither count, but the numbers show what each one is. v1 matches Nansen's published distinct signers, and v3 stays within 2% of v1 plus meta_accounts, so it also counts users whose transactions a relayer signs. The gap is therefore the size of relayed activity: about 1.8x now, but 9x to 18x in monthly averages before 2026-04-02, when a Kai-Ching batch of about 2,000,000 accounts a day stopped and a Sweat key migration ended. See docs/USAGE_BREAK_2026-04-02.md. Gold keeps both and calls neither of them daily active users.
- **`circulating_supply_chain_vs_published_schedule`**: The Launch row differs by design. NEAR's post warns that unclaimed tokens, over 300M at the time, sat in unlocked accounts until claimed, so the chain shows more unlocked than the plan until those lockups were made in the following weeks. NearBlocks' figure for that day is within 0.03% of the chain-built one. The later rows stay within 5%: the plan assumes full issuance and no burning, and lockups kept being created after launch.
- **`circulating_supply_latest_nearblocks_vs_coinmetrics`**: Both figures are retired, and they were never independent. NearBlocks computed circulating supply from lockup contracts until its 2025-09-03 rewrite removed that job, so /v1/charts and /v1/stats have read 1,249,836,992 NEAR since 2025-08-27. CoinMetrics takes project-reported supply through CoinGecko, and NEAR's report is NearBlocks' legacy endpoint: the two stayed within 0.02% (median) from November 2024, CoinMetrics carried the frozen figure for two months, and on 2025-10-25, two days after NearBlocks pointed that endpoint at total supply, it jumped to total supply and has followed the previous day's total ever since. circulating_supply_near is now built from the lockup contracts on chain: see docs/CIRCULATING_SUPPLY.md.
- **`circulating_supply_year_mean_chain_vs_nearblocks`**: Only 2024 differs, and the fault is NearBlocks'. From 2024-08-12 its job counted a lockup it failed to read three times as nothing locked, so RPC failures pushed its figure as high as 99.95% of total supply. Its commit of 2024-11-04 made such a failure stop the job instead, so the figure stood still until 2024-12-28. Outside that window the two are close every day: see the data test circulating_supply_tracks_nearblocks.
- **`intents_revenue_all_time_defillama_vs_page_monthly`**: Open question. DefiLlama matches wallet transfers within 0.1%, and the page's own wallet table matches them within 0.3%, but the page's monthly series totals about 697k NEAR and $1.71M more. About 141k NEAR of that falls in Aug to Oct 2025, before the wallets received anything; most later months add more. NEAR does not publish how this series is built, so it may count revenue when accrued rather than when swept. Treat the wallet-based figure as the checkable one.
- **`intents_volume_2026_apr_aug_defillama_vs_page`**: DefiLlama's Intents volume changes meaning on 2026-09-01. From 2026-04-01 to 2026-08-31 it matches NearBlocks' public-only figure within 0.2% (see intents_volume_2026_apr_aug_defillama_vs_nearblocks), and from 2026-09-01 it equals revenue.near.org's all-routes figure, which includes confidential Intents. Its adapter carries no date rule, so the likely cause is that DefiLlama kept its stored daily values when NEAR restated the earlier months; that is not confirmed. Read all-routes volume from volume_usd_revenue_page and public volume from volume_usd_nearblocks, and never read a trend across 2026-09-01 from volume_usd_defillama.
- **`treasury_share_30d_config_vs_epoch_rewards`**: The RPC config field reads 0, but treasury.near received EpochRewards in every epoch through this snapshot, close to 10% of issuance. Trust the observed flow, not the config field. Earlier project notes that called the treasury share 0% were wrong.
- **`tvl_latest_defillama_vs_nearblocks`**: Different inclusion rules. DefiLlama sums protocol TVL on NEAR; NearBlocks reports what it calls signer TVL, about a third of that. Neither is wrong, so state which one a chart uses.
