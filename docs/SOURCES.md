# Data sources

The table of sources, limits and terms lives in [`registry/sources.csv`](../registry/sources.csv), so it can be queried like everything else. This page records what that table cannot: how each source behaves, what is known to be wrong with it, and what was tried and rejected.

Sources change, so each note says the day its behaviour was observed, and a figure or a limit described here is true of that day. Each nightly run records what it fetched in `data/bronze/_manifest/` and how each dataset went in `data/bronze/_runs/`.

## How independent are the sources?

This matters more than how many sources agree.

| Family | Sources | Upstream |
|---|---|---|
| NEAR's own uploads | revenue.near.org, the Dune Intents dashboard, DefiLlama's Intents fees and revenue | NEAR uploads datasets to Dune. DefiLlama's adapter reads them, and its source says it mirrors revenue.near.org. Their close agreement proves little. |
| The chain, read directly | RPC, archival RPC, Neardata | Independent |
| The chain, indexed by others | NearBlocks, FastNear | Independent of NEAR's uploads, but each is one company's indexer. Public Intents only. |
| The chain, loaded by NEAR into BigQuery | NEAR's public BigQuery dataset | NEAR's own lakehouse loads it, so it shares nothing with NearBlocks' indexer or with NEAR's Dune uploads. It holds the public chain only: no confidential Intents. |
| Markets | CoinMetrics, Coinbase, DefiLlama prices | Independent of the chain |
| The Bitwise NEAR ETF's own numbers | Bitwise's page, SoSoValue's reported flows | SoSoValue's flows work out to Bitwise's share changes times NAV, so they are not a second opinion. The chain is. |
| NEAR's circulating supply as others report it | NearBlocks' `/v1` figures, CoinMetrics' estimated market cap | CoinMetrics takes project-reported supply through CoinGecko, and NEAR's report is NearBlocks' legacy endpoint. They agree because they are one number. The lockup contracts on chain are the independent read: see [CIRCULATING_SUPPLY.md](CIRCULATING_SUPPLY.md). |

Confidential Intents appear only in the NEAR family. Nothing here checks them independently, so treat them as self-reported.

The strongest checks are the ones that cross families. See [RECONCILIATION.md](RECONCILIATION.md), where `independence` marks each comparison.

## Notes per source

### NearBlocks
- v3 daily endpoints return at most 365 days per call. `limit` counts days, not rows, and `date=` returns a single day. History therefore grows with each stored snapshot.
- Legacy `/v1/charts` is the only full daily history back to 2020-07-21, and NearBlocks switches `/v1` and `/v2` off on 2026-11-22 ([migration guide](https://api.nearblocks.io/api-docs/migration.md), read 2026-11-08). The bronze copy keeps its history. Gold reads `/v3` first wherever `/v3` serves the same count: `/v3/block-stats` carries gas fee, gas used and total supply, and `/v3/price-stats` the daily price, all equal to `/v1` to the unit on every day both hold. `new_accounts`, `deleted_accounts`, `unique_contracts` and `active_signers` have no `/v3` twin and end on that date.
- v3 `stats.circulating_supply` equals total supply, and NearBlocks chose that: a comment in its [stats job](https://github.com/Nearblocks/nearblocks/blob/main/apps/backend/src/services/stats/stats.ts) says all genesis-locked tokens were released by October 2025. The chain agrees about genesis tokens, but later lockups still hold about 2M NEAR.
- `/v1` circulating supply is frozen: it has read 1,249,836,992 NEAR on every day since 2025-08-27, on `/v1/charts` and `/v1/stats` alike, because NearBlocks' [rewrite of 2025-09-03](https://github.com/Nearblocks/nearblocks/commit/f70a80f58e) removed the job behind it. Until then that job read the lockup contracts, so its history is a good test of ours: from 2024-08-12 to 2024-12-28 it was failing and reads too high, and on all other days through 2025-08-26 it matches within 0.4%, with a median gap of 0.0001%. `dq_reconciliation` compares it with the chain-built figure, and the test `circulating_supply_tracks_nearblocks` holds the two together. `/v1/legacy/circulating-supply` has returned total supply [since 2025-10-23](https://github.com/Nearblocks/nearblocks/commit/ccff1f23f3), and it answers `format=coingecko`, which is how the figure reaches CoinMetrics.
- v1 and v3 `active_accounts` are undocumented, and the gap between them is not stable: about 1.8x in October 2026 and 2.1x over the 30 days to 2026-11-11, but 9x to 18x in monthly averages from October 2025 to March 2026. The numbers show what each counts. v1 matches Nansen's published distinct signers, and v3 stays within 2% of v1 plus `meta_accounts`, so it also counts users whose transactions a relayer signs. Gold keeps both and calls neither "daily active users". `txns` and v3 cannot be compared across 2026-04-02: see [USAGE_BREAK_2026-04-02.md](USAGE_BREAK_2026-04-02.md).
- Prices are stamped at 00:00 UTC, which makes the value on day D the close of day D-1. Gold moves them back one day, after which they match the other price sources within about 0.1%.
- `tvl-stats` is a different measure from DefiLlama's chain TVL, about a third of it.
- From 2026-11-03 almost all Intents volume settles confidentially, which NearBlocks cannot see. Its public volume fell from about 60% of all-route volume in late October to between 0.4% and 18% from 2026-11-03 to 11-08, and its swaps, accounts, tokens and chains fell with it, while all-route volume held at $88M to $155M a day. NEAR's own dashboard on Dune shows the same fall in public volume, in its daily volume by Intents type, so the counts are right. NearBlocks' public volume ran about 4% above NEAR's public figure over the 30 days of the 2026-11-01 reading; `intents_volume_30d_nearblocks_vs_dune_public` holds the two together. No announcement dated to that day was found. Confidential Intents became generally available on 2026-07-08, and an integrator of the 1Click API turns it on with one parameter.
- Three captures were added on 2026-11-10, the first day with their data. They are saved to bronze and no model reads them yet. `/v3/validators` lists every staking pool with its delegator count, fee, stake and country code (862 pools on 2026-11-10, read 100 at a time); operators' contact details, links and free text are left out before saving. `/v3/validators/info` gives the seat price, the last epoch's yield and total stake. `/v3/fts/<token>/stats/transfers` gives daily transfers, amount moved and unique senders and receivers for each token in `registry/tokens.csv`; it returns only the newest 365 days and takes no date, so that history can only grow from the first night read. NearBlocks shows only the current validator state, but a missed night can still be read from each pool's own views at a past block.
- All 16 MPC operators show `tee.status = none` at NearBlocks, while NEAR says Chain Signatures run inside TEEs. A blank here may only mean NearBlocks has no attestation data. Check the contract before claiming anything.

### DefiLlama
- Intents fees and revenue come from NEAR's Dune datasets, not an independent count. Read the [adapter](https://github.com/DefiLlama/dimension-adapters/blob/master/fees/near-intents/index.ts) before citing them.
- Revenue means NEAR and wNEAR swept into three wallets, excluding hops between them. Sweeps are periodic, so a single day's revenue can exceed that day's fees.
- Its Intents volume and fees change scope during 2026. Volume matches NearBlocks' public-only figure within 0.2% from 2026-04-01 to 2026-08-31 and equals revenue.near.org's all-routes figure from 2026-09-01. Fees follow public routes only from 2026-04-01 to 2026-09-13: they run below the page on 152 of 153 days from April to August, and the daily shortfall tracks confidential volume (correlation 0.76, about 5 bps of it). The adapter has no date rule, so DefiLlama probably kept its stored values when NEAR restated those months; that is not confirmed. So `fct_intents_daily` takes all-route volume and fees from revenue.near.org, with DefiLlama only on the days the page lacks, and `dq_reconciliation` holds DefiLlama's own series. Two checks in [RECONCILIATION.md](RECONCILIATION.md) hold the volume change.
- A day's Intents fees and revenue can change until the read three nights after the day ended, a night behind revenue.near.org. `fct_intents_daily.is_fee_revenue_provisional` marks those days. Three nights is what was measured, not a guarantee: on the read of 2026-11-13, one night after this pin, DefiLlama moved its fees for 2026-11-09 by 0.026% ($263,740 to $263,808) on the fourth night. Gold takes that day's fees from revenue.near.org ($263,801), so no figure at this pin rests on the moved number, but the nightly build of that day reports the move as a failed check until the setting is raised.
- Its daily prices are stamped near midnight UTC, up to about 2.4 hours either side, and about half land a few seconds after it. Silver rounds each to the nearest midnight before taking the day. Until 2026-11-09 it took the raw timestamp's day, which dated half the points a day late and left 239 to 353 days per coin empty; TON's price in `fct_peer_daily`, which only DefiLlama gives, was wrong on those days. Rounded, its NEAR price is within 0.12% to 0.49% of CoinMetrics at the median in every year.
- The near-intents endpoint also serves `dailyHoldersRevenue`, `dailyProtocolRevenue` and `dailySupplySideRevenue`. They are arithmetic of fees and revenue (revenue split at 2026-02-23, and fees minus revenue), so they are not fetched after 2026-11-08.
- `/overview/fees/<chain>` sums the applications on a chain and leaves the chain's own transaction fees out. On 2026-11-10 NEAR's 30-day total there, $6,421,642, was its protocol rows without the Near row: $6,578,848 less $157,206. On 2026-11-08 it read $211,521 for NEAR, $194,787 of it NEAR Intents, beside $2,472 of gas. So `fct_peer_daily.fees_usd` and `revenue_usd` are application figures, not everything users paid on the chain. The chain's own fees come from its adapter at `/summary/fees/<chain>` and are `gas_fees_usd`, first fetched on 2026-11-10. The same route serves protocols, so a reply whose `protocolType` is not `chain` is refused. NEAR's adapter sums transaction fees from Allium's index, so it is a second indexer beside NearBlocks: the two are within 0.2% in every year from 2020, and `near_gas_fees_30d_defillama_vs_nearblocks` holds them together. The adapter still books 70% of those fees as revenue, the rate before protocol version 87, so its revenue series is not used.
- The fees and revenue DefiLlama lists for NEAR settle on the same schedule, because they are mostly Intents fees; `fct_peer_daily.is_fee_revenue_provisional` marks them. Other chains settle less cleanly. First reads of fees on Ethereum, Solana and Sui ran up to about 6% low, and the 2026-11-04 and 11-05 reads moved 165 and 557 of Solana's fee days, back to 2025-04-09, by up to 5.6%.
- TVL history can be rewritten on any night, without notice, so a TVL chart reproduces only at a pin. Of the five re-reads to 2026-11-05, the 11-03 read moved 2,079 Ethereum and Solana days back to 2021 (median 0.19%, largest 11.4%), the 11-02 read moved 8 recent Aptos days by 0.23%, and the other three moved nothing by more than 0.01%. NEAR's chain TVL and the protocol TVL on NEAR did not move at all.
- `bridges.llama.fi` returns HTTP 402 and is not used. `api.llama.fi` serves a bridges list without a key, at `/overview/bridges` and `/summary/bridges/<slug>` (first read on 2026-11-12), and four services in `fct_swap_services_daily` come from it.
- Three DefiLlama lists hold services that move tokens across chains, and they count different things (read on 2026-11-12). `dexs` is the swaps a service settled; NEAR Intents is in it, with THORChain and Chainflip. `bridges` is what was sent into a service on one chain (`dailyOutgoingVolume`) and what it delivered on another (`dailyIncomingVolume`). A service's daily series there is the outgoing one, so each transfer counts once: on Across it equals `dailyOutgoingVolume` on all 1,465 days, while the per-chain breakdown counts a transfer leaving and arriving and sums to twice the total. It counts a transfer that delivers the same token, which is a move and not a swap. `bridge-aggregators` is what a front end routed through other services, and is not used. The fetch names the data type it wants, so a change of DefiLlama's default cannot change what a series means, and it refuses a reply that carries another id than the registry's.
- `fct_swap_services_daily` sets NEAR Intents beside six services, listed in `registry/swap_services.csv` and chosen on 2026-11-12 by one rule. DefiLlama serves a daily dollar series for the service without a key. Its methodology for that series describes swaps or orders the service itself settles across chains: swaps through its own pools (THORChain, Chainflip), or requests, orders or deposits that a solver, relayer or taker fills on the destination chain (Relay, Across, Mayan, deBridge). The series holds at least 365 days. And its 365 days to 2026-11-11 came to at least a tenth of NEAR Intents' $33.88B, because a service under a tenth of its size is not a candidate for being as large. The registry keeps each service's figure at selection.
- What the rule left out, with the year's volume DefiLlama listed on 2026-11-12. Thirteen series in `bridges` pass the size floor and count something else: deposits into a bridge that locks or burns a token and releases or mints the same token on another chain, or into one exchange's or one chain's own bridge. They are Circle CCTP ($133.1B), LayerZero V2 ($101.5B), USDT0 ($60.8B), Hyperliquid Bridge ($52.3B), Coinbase Bridge ($22.9B, cbBTC redeemed), CCIP ($16.3B), Portal ($16.1B), Lighter Bridge ($13.0B), Polygon Bridge ($7.9B), Arbitrum Bridge ($5.5B), Stargate V2 ($5.1B), Circle Gateway ($4.6B) and Optimism Bridge ($3.5B). Unit ($7.7B, in `dexs` under the category Bridge) is spot trading on Hyperliquid in the tokens Unit issued. Maya Protocol ($1.3B), a THORChain fork, is under the floor, as are Rhino.fi, Butter Network, Meson and Symbiosis. Adding a service is one row in the registry.
- Aggregators are left out for three reasons. They settle nothing themselves, so their volume is already in the services they route through. NEAR Intents is one of those services: in the 30 days to 2026-11-11 the Explorer tally has `lifi` at $125.5M and `rango` at $52.1M of 1Click swaps (`fct_intents_1click_channel_daily`). And `bridge-aggregators` has no one definition: four of the six above the floor publish no methodology (LI.FI $7.2B, Jumper $9.0B, Bitget Wallet $4.1B, Rango $3.5B), Socket ($5.2B) counts same-chain swaps too from 2026-06-11, and Silentswap ($6.0B) is read from its own reporter contract.
- The services' series hold zeros that this warehouse cannot explain: THORChain from 2026-05-16 to 06-21 and on 08-27, Mayan from 2026-08-08 to 08-30, Chainflip on 2026-09-13 and 09-14, Relay on 2025-10-02 and 10-03. THORChain's and Mayan's long runs each have a low day on either side, which is how a halt would look, but nothing here confirms one. Gold keeps the zeros as served. Across has no value for 2026-10-29 and Mayan none for 2026-10-22. Every series comes from the service's own index or API through DefiLlama's adapter, so none is an independent count.
- `/protocols` and `/protocol/<slug>` are large, so they are trimmed to NEAR before saving, and the manifest note records that. `/protocols` also lists exchange wallets on NEAR, which gold marks with `is_cex` and leaves out of TVL.
- A protocol's `id` is what stays the same; its `slug` can change. On the 2026-11-05 read DefiLlama made Templar a parent group: the old listing, id 6570, became `templar-lending` with its whole history, and `/protocol/templar-protocol` now answers with the group, id `parent#templar-protocol`. So gold goes by `id`, and the rule is on `dim_protocol` and `fct_protocol_tvl_daily` in [DATA_CATALOG.md](DATA_CATALOG.md).
- Ingest never asks for a retired slug, because it rebuilds its list from `/protocols` each night: the top 25 by TVL on NEAR. At the 2026-11-05 pin nine of those are exchange wallets that gold leaves out, so `fct_protocol_tvl_daily` holds 16 of the 47 protocols. A protocol that falls out of the 25 stays in the table and stops updating.
- Protocols in the `CEX` category are exchange wallets that DefiLlama labels on its exchange pages. Their NEAR figure is the USD value of every asset those wallets hold on NEAR, at DefiLlama's prices, and DefiLlama leaves them out of chain TVL. `fct_exchange_balance_daily` keeps them per exchange per day: nine exchanges have history at the 2026-11-05 pin, Binance's wallets alone holding about $536M on 2026-11-04. Only labelled wallets count, so an exchange's unlabelled wallets are missing, and a relabelling moves the whole series.

### revenue.near.org
- Rendered tiles show 0 in plain HTML, but the real series are embedded in the Next.js payload. `ingest/parsers/revenue_page.py` reads them and fails loudly if a series disappears.
- `protocol_fee_near`, `intents_revenue_near` and `burn_revenue_near` are 0 on every row. Only total fees carry information.
- A day's fees are published before the day's data has fully arrived and are rewritten on the next read. Three of the four days measured first read at 6% to 14% of the figure that followed ($9,319, then $164,920 for 2026-11-03). Daily volume is not rewritten. `fct_intents_daily.is_fee_revenue_provisional` marks those days.
- Three cumulative revenue figures appear on or around this page, and they do not match. See `intents_revenue_all_time_defillama_vs_page_monthly` in [RECONCILIATION.md](RECONCILIATION.md).

### FastNear
- Transfers carry a USD value at transfer time, which is FastNear's own calculation.
- The crawl walks oldest to newest with `resume_token` and resumes where it stopped.
- `intents.near` and token contracts have far too many transfers to crawl, so they use daily contract statistics instead.
- Holdings and top holders exist only for nights a snapshot ran. From 2026-11-10 top holders are kept for all twelve tokens in `registry/tokens.csv` (SWEAT, nBTC, USDT.e, USDC.e and xRHEA were added), and holdings for `v2.jars.sweat`, `claim.sweat` and House of Stake's treasury, `hos-deposits.sputnik-dao.near`, which `vote.dao get_config` names. Sweat Wallet and the SWEAT token as they exist end on 2026-12-30.
- A fee collector can have no native account at all: `7066...f05` holds balances only inside `intents.near`, so `view_account` reports it as unknown. That is expected.

### CoinMetrics
- `CapMrktEstUSD` is price times a circulating supply [reported by each project and collected through CoinGecko](https://docs.coinmetrics.io/network-data/network-data-overview/market/market-capitalization). So it means something different per asset: on 2026-11-05 the supply behind it was 41% of total for Sui, 73% for Aptos, 93% for Solana and Avalanche, and 100% for Ethereum. Solana's matches its own `getSupply` RPC figure.
- For NEAR it follows NearBlocks. It stayed within 0.02% (median) of NearBlocks' figure from November 2024, carried NearBlocks' frozen figure for two months, and on 2025-10-25, two days after NearBlocks pointed its endpoint at total supply, jumped to total supply. Since then it has equalled the previous day's total supply. Gold no longer uses it for NEAR; `dq_reconciliation` compares it with the chain-built figure.
- TON's asset id is `gram`: the reference data names it "Gram (prev. Toncoin)". The id `ton` is refused by the catalogue and returns no rows from the time series, which is why TON had no market cap in `fct_peer_daily`. `registry/peers.csv` asks for `gram` from 2026-11-10; its price starts on 2022-05-05 and its market cap on 2022-11-03, and over the 1,647 days both hold, its price is within 0.13% of DefiLlama's TON price at the median and 0.59% at the 95th percentile. Each asset's first read asks for its whole history, so a renamed or added asset is not left with the 14-day overlap.
- The community tier offers `SplyCur` and `CapMrktCurUSD` only for some assets (Ethereum among the peers, not NEAR), so there is no CoinMetrics total supply to compare with.
- `PriceUSD` is [the fixed closing price at 00:00 UTC as the day ends](https://docs.coinmetrics.io/network-data/network-data-overview/market/price), from CoinMetrics' reference rate service, and the row carries that day's date. CoinMetrics restates it in small steps. ETH and SOL from 2024-11-01, read again on 2026-11-13, differed from what the peer feed read on 2026-11-01 on 3 and 21 of 741 days, by 0.012% at most (SOL, 2026-05-31). A day older than the 14-day overlap is read once, so each feed keeps what it first saw.
- Its `wbtc` series is not a price of WBTC: on all 742 days from 2024-11-01 it equals `btc` to the last digit.

### Archival RPC
- `block`, `view_account` and `call_function` work at old heights.
- `rpc/contract_views_history` uses that to replay ten contract views at the first block of every day in `rpc/block_day`, which is daily from 2025-01-01 and the first of each month before: price, supply and staked balance of the three liquid staking contracts, supply and accounts of `venear.dao`, and the state of `v1.signer`. One run on 2026-11-10 read all 732 days in two and a half hours, and no call was refused. Each day is one file, 60 days a run, newest first, and the files themselves are the record of which days are done. From then on each run adds the new day.
- A contract has no answer before it existed, and that is kept as an answer (`UNKNOWN_ACCOUNT`), so the day is not asked again; after two such days a contract is not asked about on older ones. stNEAR answers from 2021-09-01, LiNEAR from 2022-04-01, `v1.signer` from 2024-09-01 (its `state` view fails on 2024-08-01), rNEAR from 2025-08-03 and veNEAR from 2025-10-11. The eleven monthly days before 2021-07 hold no views.
- `fct_lst_snapshot`, `fct_venear_snapshot`, `fct_mpc_state` and `fct_mpc_participants` use the replay for every date it has and the nightly read only for a date it lacks; `read_from` says which. At the 2026-11-10 pin every row is a replay. A replayed row is the state as the day began, and the nightly read is taken some hours into the day: on the ten nights that have both, the nightly LiNEAR and rNEAR price was 0.003% to 0.017% above the replay, which is those hours of staking rewards (test `contract_view_replay_agrees_with_nightly`). The two comparisons with NearBlocks, `mpc_operators_match_contract` and `mpc_threshold`, use the nightly read, because NearBlocks is read the same night.
- `v1.signer state` changed shape on 2025-04-30. Before, participants are a map from account to id with one public key, counted as one signature domain. After, they are a list, with the signature domains and a keyset beside them. Both are read. The contract had 8 participants and a threshold of 5 from 2024-09 and 16 and 11 on 2026-11-10; the second domain appears on 2025-05-01 and the fourth on 2026-03-20.
- The share price never falls in the replay, on any of the three tokens, and staked NEAR equals supply times price within one part in a million on every row. LiNEAR's price steps up by 0.18% to 0.29% on three single days (2026-08-08, 2026-09-04, 2026-09-19) against a usual 0.02% a day; that is what the contract returned.
- `rpc/intents_pool_history` reads what `intents.near` held of named tokens as each month ended, from November 2024, the contract's first month (the account has its contract at the first block of 2024-12-01 and does not exist at the first block of 2024-11-01), and as the day before each snapshot ended. Three kinds of token are read, and no contract is typed by hand: wNEAR, from the registry; the dollar tokens, which are every NEAR token the newest 1Click list prices within 2% of one dollar; and the tokens of the pool's other main assets, which are every NEAR token that list names with the symbol of an asset that holds at least 1% of the pool's value on the newest day of NEAR's dashboard series and that `registry/token_prices.csv` lists with a price. At the 2026-11-12 pin those assets are ZEC, BTC, WBTC, ETH, XLM, QTC, TLO and SOL, and with wNEAR and the dollar tokens the assets read are 92% of the pool's value on the dashboard's newest day; the other 8% is 128 assets, none above 1.1%. One asset can be several tokens, one for each chain it is bridged from: ETH is nine, and BTC is `btc.omft.near` and `nbtc.bridge.near`. The list decides, not the holdings read, so a listed token the pool has emptied is still read. A token that has left the list is not read, and neither is an asset that is under 1% of the pool today, however large it was, so the result is a history of named tokens and never the whole pool, and an early month can lack what the pool held then.
- The contract keeps public and confidential balances in its own account, so a token contract's answer about that one account is the pool's whole balance of the token. A NEP-141 token answers `ft_balance_of`. The tokens bridged through HOT are not token contracts of their own: they are NEP-245 balances inside `v2_1.omni.hot.tg`, which FastNear's holdings read does not list, so `fct_wallet_holdings` lacks them; XLM is held only this way. That contract answers `mt_batch_balance_of` for all of them in one call; at the month ends of April and May 2025 it had no such view yet and was asked token by token with `mt_balance_of`, and before April 2025 it did not exist. As 2026-11-11 ended it held $8.4M of the pool's $70.6M of dollar tokens, so a read of the token contracts alone runs about an eighth short. One wNEAR is one NEAR and a dollar token counts as one dollar; the other assets are kept in token units, and nothing here types or guesses a price. `fct_intents_pool_chain_monthly` holds the month ends and the newest day: wNEAR grew from 1,933 as November 2024 ended to 17.1M, and dollar tokens from $82K to $70.6M. Test `intents_pool_history_agrees_with_holdings` holds the wNEAR and the dollars of each reading against FastNear's holdings read of the same day.
- On 2026-11-13 the host answered about 270 of those calls at its usual pace of one every 1.5 s and then returned HTTP 429 on most. The pool job therefore waits 4 s before each call, sends a generic client name and no key, and ends its run when a call is refused twice. At that pace the rest of the history, about 770 calls, drew no refusal. A run reads at most 8 days, which is up to half an hour, and a night with nothing to catch up reads one day in about four minutes.
- `validators` by `epoch_id` answers some asks for a past epoch and returns `UNKNOWN_EPOCH` on others, and by `block_id` it returns `VALIDATOR_INFO_UNAVAILABLE`. A refusal says nothing about the epoch. The host answers from several nodes that do not hold the same epochs, and a kept-alive connection stays with one node: on one connection 316 of 688 epochs were refused, and six more asks on that connection were all refused too. Asked again on 2026-11-10, each time on a fresh connection, all 316 answered, 291 within 8 asks and the last within 16. So at the 2026-11-10 pin every day from 2025-01-01 has its epoch. The fetcher asks for the epoch in force at each day's first block, newest first, and asks a refused epoch again on later runs, up to 24 times, so `fct_validator_epoch` holds one epoch a day out of about three, plus the current epoch read each night. The epoch in force as 2025-01-01 began had started the day before, where no daily block is on file, so its row has no date. Two earlier notes here, that the node answered nothing before 2026-04-19 and later that 316 epochs were unavailable, both described this effect.
- The nightly read (`validators` with no argument) returns whichever epoch is running, so its block, chunk and endorsement counts stop where the read happened: the nine nightly epochs in bronze on 2026-11-09 had run between 1,297 and 41,236 of 43,200 blocks. Stakes and the validator set are fixed when an epoch starts, so those are right either way. `is_epoch_complete` marks the difference, from the validators' expected blocks adding up to the epoch length. The backfill now reads each nightly epoch again by its id once a later night shows a newer one, and saves nothing from an epoch still in progress, so an archival read always carries final counts (test `validator_archival_reads_are_complete`). Those nine were read again on 2026-11-10, so at that pin only the newest epoch, the one in progress, is partial.
- The validator count swings from one epoch to the next and that is the chain, not the reads: epochs 4944, 4947, 4948 and 4950 returned 335, 352, 339 and 352 validators when read again after they ended (2026-11-10), with 12 to 25 kicked out each time. Pools that are offline are removed and come back on their standing stake. Prefer stake-weighted measures to the raw count.
- Calls hit HTTP 429 quickly. The free RPC host recognises a FastNear key, but the archival host shows no sign of it, so archival calls keep the keyless pace.
- `EXPERIMENTAL_protocol_config` by `block_id` gives the config in force at a past block, so a change can be dated to the block, while the nightly config read dates it only to the night. That is how `registry/protocol_upgrades.csv` dates protocol version 87, which removed the developer gas rebate: block 223012336 (11:46:28 UTC on 2026-11-04) reads version 86 and a 3/10 contract reward, and block 223012337, the first of a new epoch, reads 87 and 0. The block header's `latest_protocol_version` already read 87 that morning, so it cannot date a change.

### Token metadata (`rpc/ft_metadata`)
- Asks each token seen with a positive balance in `fastnear/account_full`, or seen in `fastnear/ft_top_holders`, what its contract says through `ft_metadata`. 7,933 tokens had been seen by 2026-11-05, nearly all held by `dclv2.ref-labs.near`, `v2.ref-finance.near` and `intents.near`.
- Each token is read once. Answers are kept in `data/state/rpc__ft_metadata.json`, and each run's new answers in one bronze file. An answer whose bronze file has gone is read again, because the nightly job drops its own bronze dated up to `main`'s pin, so a run on the pin's day would otherwise lose those tokens for good.
- A night makes at most 2,000 calls, tokens with the most rows first, so the backlog takes four nights and later nights ask only about new tokens. At the keyed pace of 0.15 s a call that is about 5 minutes, but this pace has only been seen on runs of about 50 calls, so check the first night's time in `data/bronze/_runs/`.
- Without a key, one call a second drew HTTP 429 after about 3 minutes, and 300 tokens took 412 seconds. A run stops after 40 minutes, which limits a keyless run to about 1,750 tokens and keeps a node that rate-limits every call from stretching the night past the job's timeout.
- `icon` and `reference` are dropped, because an icon can be a large image. `spec`, `name` and `symbol` are cut to printable characters and to 32, 128 and 64 characters, because a contract can put anything there.
- Some answers are final and are kept: `UNKNOWN_ACCOUNT` (the contract is gone), `INVALID_ACCOUNT`, `NO_CONTRACT_CODE`, `CONTRACT_EXECUTION_ERROR` (no such method, or it panicked), `NOT_METADATA` (it returned something else) and `BAD_DECIMALS` (not a whole number from 0 to 255). These tokens stay empty in gold.
- Any other error says something about the node, not the token, so the token is asked again on a later run, after the tokens that have not failed yet. Three failures in a row, or one call that used up its retries, end the run for that night.
- Name and symbol are the contract's own claim: `usdc-4d51ec.launchpad.justhoot.near` calls itself USDC, with 18 decimals. `fct_wallet_holdings.metadata_source` shows which rows rest on a claim. Group by `token_id`, and treat these fields as untrusted text when displaying them.
- Until a token's contract has been asked, the decimals of the list that prices it stand in (`metadata_source` reads `price_list`). A list's price is per whole token counted with its own decimals, so the two belong together, and the lists matched the contract on every token read in the sample below. A contract that has answered is final, even with an error, so no list fills in behind it. The test `token_decimals_lists_match_contracts` fails if a list and a contract ever disagree. This is what lets `fct_wallet_holdings` carry values at the 2026-11-05 pin, before the first night of `ft_metadata` reads.
- A sample on 2026-11-05, not stored: of 300 tokens picked at random, 296 gave decimals, 2 no longer exist and 2 are `mock-ft` contracts that panic. So about 99% of holdings rows should convert once the backlog is read. All 12 registry tokens' decimals matched their contracts, and so did all 30 tokens read that 1Click lists. `token_decimals_match_ft_metadata` holds the registry to the contracts from now on.

### BigQuery public dataset
`bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us` is the dataset the hackathon's sample prompt names: every block, transaction and receipt since genesis, loaded by NEAR's own lakehouse. It was connected on 2026-11-14, and every figure below was read that day. `ingest/sources/bigquery.py` reads it through BigQuery's REST API, and each query is one file in [`ingest/sql/bigquery/`](../ingest/sql/bigquery), run word for word.

- **Access.** BigQuery has no keyless route: a query runs as a job in a Google Cloud project of the reader's own. The loader needs `GOOGLE_APPLICATION_CREDENTIALS`, the path of a key file for a service account with the role BigQuery Job User, and the `google-auth` package (`pip install -r requirements-bigquery.txt`). Without them the datasets are skipped. `make all`, `make warehouse` and `make verify` need neither, because the results are in bronze like every other source's.
- **It is live.** NEAR's documentation warns that NEAR Lake, the feed the dataset's published loader reads, was switched off on 2026-03-24, and that loader's repository has not changed since 2025, so freshness was the first thing tested. At 13:07 UTC on 2026-11-14 the `blocks`, `transactions` and `receipt_actions` tables had all been written within the hour and held rows for that day. `dq_bigquery_tables` records each table's rows, size and last change on every read: `transactions` holds 5.49 billion rows and `receipt_actions` 23.3 billion.
- **The dataset is in `us-east1`.** A job told to run in `US` is refused with "does not have permission to query table, or perhaps it does not exist", which reads like a permissions fault and is not one. The loader names no location and lets BigQuery place the job with the tables.
- **Only counts are stored.** The seven queries return 73,402 rows, and bronze holds 1.26 MB with the two files of a first, narrower pass: blocks and total supply per day since genesis; transactions, distinct signers and receivers per day from 2025-01-01, with each day's 25 largest receivers; actions per day by kind and each day's 25 largest contracts by calls, signers and callers from 2026-04-03, the day after the batch streams stopped; rows per day in every table since genesis; NEAR's own daily circulating supply; signers by the month they first signed; and the list of tables. No single transaction is copied, and no account outside a day's 25 largest leaves BigQuery. `BIGQUERY_FIRST_DAY_<QUERY>` starts a first read earlier.
- **Cost.** Google bills a query by the bytes of the columns it reads, and the first 1,000 GB of a month are free. The reads of 2026-11-14 were billed 470.9 GB in all: 155.4 for the signer cohorts, 126.2 for transactions, 67.5 for actions, 11.8 for blocks, almost nothing for the rest, and 110.1 for a first, narrower pass that the wider one replaced. Before a query runs the loader asks BigQuery what it would read, which is free, and it does not run a query over 150 GB or take a month past 600 GB on its own count; the cohort query is over the first limit and runs only with the limit raised for that run. `dq_bigquery_reads` lists every read in bronze with its SQL file's hash and what it was billed, and `data/state/bigquery_bytes.json` keeps the month's total.
- **How the reads are filed.** They were taken on 2026-11-14 and saved in the 2026-11-12 snapshot, the one the build is pinned to, with their windows ending on 2026-11-11, the pin's last full day. The manifest's `fetched_at` and each file's `read_at` give the true time. Where two reads of a day sit in one snapshot, the later one is used. The partition records and the supply table also held 12 to 14 November by then; silver leaves out any day on or after the snapshot's own.
- **It agrees with NearBlocks almost to the unit.** Over the 680 days from 2025-01-01 to 2026-11-11 the two count exactly the same distinct signers on every day and exactly the same transactions on 678. On the other two, 2025-01-23 and 2025-08-20, BigQuery has about 200,000 more transactions and the same signers. So NearBlocks' `/v1` active accounts is `COUNT(DISTINCT signer_account_id)` and nothing else. Blocks are equal on 2,297 of the 2,305 days since genesis, and total supply at the day's last block is within one part in a million of the archival node's on all 2,305. The chain counts on the site are NearBlocks'; BigQuery is the check on them, in eighteen rows of `dq_reconciliation`.
- **Older days differ a little.** BigQuery records how many rows each table holds per day, which costs nothing to read and covers every day since genesis. Against NearBlocks the transactions table's rows are equal on 1,833 of 2,292 days: every day of 2026 and 363 of 365 in 2025. Over all days the two are 0.07% apart. From 2020 to 2022 NearBlocks is higher by a handful of transactions on most of the days that differ; in 2023 and 2024 BigQuery is higher on 93 days, and 2024-06-17 alone holds 3.0 million of its 3.9 million extra rows. Neither source says how it loaded old days, so this is recorded as open.
- **A signer is not a user.** `signer_account_id` is the account that signed and paid. When a relayer signs for a user, the relayer is the signer and the user's account is the transaction's receiver. In `receipt_actions` the user is the receipt's predecessor, so `fct_bigquery_contract_daily` carries both: `signers`, and `callers`, which sees the users a relayer paid for and also counts contracts calling the contract. A relayed request is a `DELEGATE_ACTION`: its daily count equals NearBlocks' meta-transactions on 180 of 223 days and is 0.0002% apart in total, and the accounts it was sent to are NearBlocks' meta accounts.
- **Accounts by contract, which no other source here gives.** On 2026-11-11, 75,823 accounts signed a transaction that led to a function call, and 73,693 of them, 97.2%, called `game.hot.tg`, HOT's game. It had 155,956 distinct callers, 83% of the day's. That is the rule, not one day: `game.hot.tg` had the most signers on every one of the 223 days read, with a median share of 98.1% and a lowest of 78.7%. `intents.near`, which carries most of the chain's fee revenue, had 221 signers and 508 callers on 2026-11-11; NearBlocks reports 510 callers for it that day, and the two are 0.3% apart over 223 days.
- **Most accounts sign once.** `fct_bigquery_signer_cohort_monthly` places each of the 74.9 million accounts that ever signed a transaction in the month it first signed, and counts it again in each later month it signed in. 46.9 million of them first signed in 2024 and 19.3 million in 2023; December 2023 is the largest month, with 12.4 million. Of the accounts that first signed in 2024, 39.7% signed again the next month and 4.1% twelve months later. Of those that first signed from January to September 2026, 5.6% signed again the next month, and of the January to April ones 1.2% six months later. In October 2026, 826,700 accounts signed a transaction, and 729,811 of them, 88%, had first signed before 2026: the chain's active accounts are mostly old ones, not new arrivals. An account also signs its own deletion, and a batch that makes accounts to use once fills a month with accounts that were never going to return, so a cohort has to be read beside what happened that month.
- **NearBlocks' active contracts is not a count of contracts.** BigQuery finds about 1,100 distinct accounts receiving a function call a day since April 2026. NearBlocks' `active_contracts` averages about 84,000 over the same days and moves with the accounts a relayer signed for (correlation 0.9), not with the contracts called (correlation -0.25). Nothing on the site reads it.
- **What it does not reproduce.** NearBlocks' `new_accounts` cannot be rebuilt from `CREATE_ACCOUNT`: over 223 days BigQuery counts 605,047 accounts created by that action against NearBlocks' 3,205,592, because most accounts are implicit ones that exist from their first transfer. Accounts receiving a `DEPLOY_CONTRACT` equal NearBlocks' `new_contracts_v1` on 83 of 223 days and are a quarter lower in total, so that figure counts something more. Deleted accounts do line up: 0.05% apart in total.
- **NEAR's own circulating supply confirms this warehouse's.** The dataset holds a `circulating_supply` table that NEAR's lakehouse computes each day (`fct_bigquery_circulating_supply_daily`, from 2023-01-01). The two series are computed at different blocks of the day, so the comparison is on the NEAR each counts as locked. On 2026-11-11 NEAR's table gives 2,095,654 locked and this warehouse, from every lockup contract read on chain, 2,095,630. On 1,111 of the 1,118 days NEAR's table holds a figure the two are within 0.1%, with a median gap of 75 NEAR; the largest gaps are 3.5% on 2024-04-18 and 2.7% on 2025-08-29. NEAR's table reports a circulating supply of zero on 291 days in a row, from 2024-05-10 to 2025-02-24: a fault in the table, marked `is_zero_in_source` and left without a figure.
- **Not read.** The token and NFT event tables, `execution_outcomes`, `account_changes` and `transaction_actions`. No chart on the site reads the BigQuery tables yet.
- **It runs where a key is.** The nightly GitHub job has no Google key, so these tables grow only when someone with a key runs `python -m ingest run --sources bigquery`. `dq_freshness` gives them no limit for that reason. The cohort query reads the whole history each time and is in the `backfill` profile, started by hand.
- **Terms.** NEAR's documentation offers the dataset for public use and states no licence for it. What is kept is public chain data reduced to counts, credited to the dataset.

### Neardata
- A block is about 265 KB, so raw blocks are counted and thrown away.
- The published limit is 180 requests per minute, but three parallel workers hit HTTP 429 from this machine. The sampler runs one request at a time.
- Sample results are estimates. `fct_receiver_sample_daily` keeps `blocks_sampled` on every row.

### GitHub
- `stats/commit_activity` and `stats/contributors` are GitHub's own weekly statistics for the last 52 weeks. For a repository with over 10,000 commits they answer HTTP 200 with a year of zeros and an empty contributor list: `anza-xyz/agave` (34,937 commits) on every read. Gold does not use the statistic: `fct_dev_activity_weekly` counts the default branch for every repository, and `dq_reconciliation` compares the two for near/nearcore. `stats/contributors` is no longer fetched: nothing read it, since authors per week come from the commits list. The nights already fetched stay in bronze.
- The statistic also counts more than the default branch holds. For `near/nearcore` it gave 1,749 commits for the 51 full weeks to 2026-10-31, while the commits list, the search API and GraphQL's `history` all give 1,345 on `master`; the difference is presumably commits on release branches, since a sweep of one week found such commits on `2.14-release`. So `github/commits_weekly` counts the default branch from the commits list for every repository, by committer date, with authors reduced to a count: `fct_dev_activity_weekly.commits_default_branch` and `authors_default_branch`, one method for every repository and the only figures for Agave (4,303 commits on `master` over the 51 full weeks to 2026-10-31). The statistic runs both above and below the default branch across the 18 repositories (MystenLabs/sui 5,067 against 2,823; ton-blockchain/ton 980 against 1,267), so the two measures are kept apart and never mixed in one series. It costs one call per 100 commits, about 250 calls a night across the 18 repositories, so without a token only the repositories whose statistics came back as zeros are read.
- Weeks start on Sunday UTC, as GitHub cuts them. The week in progress at a read is left out until a later read sees it end.

### Liquid staking contracts
- Price, supply and staked NEAR are read from each contract: `linear-protocol.near` and `lst.rhealab.near` answer `ft_price`, `ft_total_supply` and `get_total_staked_balance`, and `meta-pool.near` answers `get_contract_state`. rNEAR's `get_total_staked_balance` was added to the nightly read on 2026-11-10 (it answered 18.80M NEAR that day); before the first night with it, `fct_lst_snapshot.delegated_near` for rNEAR is supply times price, which is the same figure because a contract's price is its staked NEAR divided by its supply. On LiNEAR and stNEAR the two were under one part in a million apart over nine nights, and test `lst_delegated_matches_supply_times_price` holds them within 0.01%.
- Meta Pool's Prometheus metrics (`validators.narwallets.com/metrics`) were fetched until 2026-11-10 and read by nothing, so the fetch and its parser are gone. The nights already fetched stay in bronze.

### Intents Explorer API
- Covers 1Click swaps only, despite being the track's suggested volume source. On 2026-10-31 that was about 30% of all Intents volume. For total Intents volume, use `fct_intents_daily`.
- Rows hold users' wallet addresses and run to about 14,000 a day, and raw pages would add about 450 KB per 1,000 rows. So each UTC day is walked once, counted and thrown away, and only the daily tally is stored.
- The date filter's bounds are exclusive and can reach just past the day, so rows are checked against the day as well. Pagination follows the last row's deposit address and memo.
- Statuses can still change, so the last 3 days are tallied again each night.
- The limit is 1 request per 5 seconds. A nightly run walks 600 pages in this order: the last 60 days in full, then one day a week back to 2024-11, then the days between, newest first. The whole history had been walked by 2026-11-10, after a run on a local machine went through the rest back to back, so each 1Click table holds every day with a swap from 2025-03-27. A day with no swap leaves no row, and ten days from 2025-04-05 to 2025-05-18 had none. `is_in_daily_run` marks the unbroken run after the newest missing day (from 2025-05-19 at the 2026-11-10 pin): where a sum must not skip a day, use only rows where it is true. Test `oneclick_daily_run_has_no_gaps` holds the flag.
- A few USD amounts arrive in another notation than the usual plain decimal: `18,505.12` on 2025-11-24, with thousands separators, and `544,8666` and `22,1775` on 2025-10-31, with a decimal comma. Each reading was checked against the row's token amount and that day's price for the token (544.97 USDT; 0.118 SOL at $186.57). The fetcher reads those two forms. One comma followed by exactly three digits could be either, so that form and any other leave the day untallied, with a warning, until someone has looked.
- A swap's USD figure is trustworthy only once it has finished. For a refunded, failed or processing swap the Explorer reports the amount asked for, which nothing checks: one refunded swap on 2026-07-28 reads $29.99 trillion and 245 on 2026-09-15 read $19.3 billion together, so a sum over every status came to $30.0 trillion against $6.5 billion of finished swaps. `fct_intents_1click_channel_daily` therefore keeps USD for `SUCCESS` rows only and the count for the rest; silver keeps the tallies as fetched. Test `oneclick_usd_only_on_finished_swaps` holds this.
- The rows carry no sender before 2026, so `fct_intents_1click_daily.senders` is empty there, not 0.
- `depositType` and `recipientType` say how a user's funds came in and went out, not where the swap settled. From 2026-11-03 1Click moved more volume each day than all public Intents together, so most of it settled confidentially while its legs still read `ORIGIN_CHAIN` and `DESTINATION_CHAIN`. Do not read the route table as a public and confidential split.
- `appFees` entries are read as basis points of the value paid in. A fee recipient is named only if `registry/entities.csv` lists it; the rest are summed as `(other)`.
- A 1Click API key adds no market-wide data, so no fetcher uses one. Its quote and status endpoints serve a partner's own swaps, and account history needs a user session and an invitation.

### Dune
- Everything from Dune is derived from the [NEAR Intents dashboard](https://dune.com/near/near-intents), created by @near, and says so: gold tables carry a `derived_from` column, and each manifest note repeats it. NEAR uploads the data behind the dashboard, so nothing here checks it independently.
- Results are public and readable without logging in, but [Dune's terms](https://dune.com/terms) bar bots, scrapers and access other than its own interface. They allow copying public content with credit to its creator, and they don't say whether that covers query results. So readings are dated, credited and taken on request, in a browser, and nothing fetches the dashboard on a schedule. The last note of this section says how each stored reading was taken.
- To take a reading:
  1. Open the dashboard in your own browser, with the Network tab of the developer tools open.
  2. Scroll until every widget has loaded.
  3. Page through the table "Total generated fees by channel" to its last page. It shows 25 of its 100 rows at a time and asks for each page separately.
  4. Save the Network tab as a HAR file, using the sanitized option if your browser offers it.
  5. Run `python -m tools.record_dune_reading <file.har> <date> "<how it was read>"`. See how many widgets were saved, and how many were partial. Tiles ask for one row of a longer result and table pages for 25 rows, so those count as partial.
- The tool keeps each widget's response exactly as received, and leaves headers, cookies and the page's signature behind. A table page after the first is kept under its row offset (`__offset-25__`). A widget asked for twice keeps its fullest response, so a tile's one-row call never replaces a full result. The date can't be later than `data/PINNED`, so move the pin first if it is. Keep the HAR file out of the repository; `.gitignore` covers it.
- `fct_dune_dashboard_results` holds every row of every widget. Widgets that matter get a typed table: `fct_dune_intents_asset_daily` and `fct_dune_intents_chain_daily` (volume per asset and per blockchain), `fct_dune_intents_swaps_daily`, `fct_dune_intents_tvl_daily` and `fct_dune_intents_tvl_asset_daily` (value held, by public or confidential balance and by asset). These cover every route, confidential ones included, which NearBlocks no longer sees. Each dollar of a swap is counted once, and the dashboard does not say on which side, so its shares are not comparable with the Explorer's, which count an asset on either side.
- `stg_dune_public__tiles` names the query and column behind each headline tile, matched on 2026-11-10 to the values the page displayed: all-time volume 5179085, 30 days 5175176, 7 days 5175173, past day 5175161, total generated fees 6162606, TVL 7586891. Each has an all, a public and a confidential column, and all equals public plus confidential to the dollar wherever all three were read. `fct_intents_fee_channels` reads query 6162721, and a reading counts only once all four pages are in.
- Each reading of the 30-day asset widget (5175131) covers about 30 days, so one reading every few weeks keeps that series continuous. The dashboard also has full-history widgets, first read on 2026-11-10: volume per asset (5157527) and per blockchain (5157565) back to December 2024, TVL per asset (7586763) and per Intents type (8644298), volume per Intents type over 30 days (8644439), fees per day (6162763) and swaps per day (4374089). They are in `fct_dune_dashboard_results`; all but the 30-day volume per Intents type and the fees per day, which repeat series the warehouse already has, also have a typed table. Tests hold the splits together: volume per blockchain and per asset add up to one figure on every day, and so do the value held per asset and per kind of balance.
- The value held per asset (7586763) is the one series here that the chain checks, at month ends, through `fct_intents_pool_chain_monthly`. The dashboard's figure for a day is the pool as that day ends: USDC, its largest dollar token, is within 0.5% of `intents.near`'s balance on chain at every month end of 2026 so far, and USDCx, USAD, USDG and USD1 within 0.3%. Two things are missing from it. It has no value for USDT0 before 2026-03-09, though the pool held $0.7M of it as January ended and $1.3M as February did, so its dollar tokens run 3% and 7% low at those two month ends. And at six of the seven month ends from April to October 2026 its USDT is about $0.5M below the chain's, which is what the pool holds of USDT bridged from Scroll inside HOT's contract. With those, its dollar tokens are within 2% of the chain's at every month end from March 2026 (`intents_pool_dollars_month_ends_chain_vs_dune`). It gives wNEAR in dollars at a price of its own: over the chain's balance that price is inside Coinbase's range for the day at all ten month ends, where the day's close would put the two up to 7% apart (`intents_pool_wnear_month_ends_dune_vs_coinbase_range`). The pool's other main assets are read from the chain in token units and are not compared with the dashboard, because that needs a price for each.
- Dune leaves out `total_row_count` when it sends a result whole, within its `request_max_result_size_bytes` (256 MB), as it did for the two largest widgets on 2026-11-10. Such a response holds every row.
- A reading's all-time tiles stand still, so `dq_reconciliation` compares them with the other sources on the reading's last full day, the day before the snapshot, which is what the tiles covered. Comparing them with the latest day drifted about 0.3% a day and failed the nightly checks on 2026-11-04 and 11-05, until this was changed.
- Free access is view-only with no API, so the official fetcher needs a key on a plan or trial with API access. It skips cleanly without one.
- `near.balances` and `near.circulating_supply` were stale as of the 2026-10 research, and a NEAR data gap ran from 2026-02-28 to 03-19. Check before querying them.
- All three stored readings were taken by an AI-driven browser at the owner's request, and their manifest notes say so. On 2026-11-01 one read the page's tiles and fee table as displayed (`dune_public/near_intents_dashboard`); the asset widget that day was copied by hand. On 2026-11-10 Claude Code's built-in browser loaded every widget without logging in and paged through the fee table, while a script in the page copied the dashboard's own requests and responses. Dune's content security policy stops a page from sending data to the local machine, so the copy came out as a browser download, a HAR file the recorder read like any other. The three all-scope headline tiles (all-time, past day, 7 days) loaded before the copy began. All-time is still read from 5179085's full series, but the past-day and 7-day tiles for all scopes have no row on 2026-11-10. On 2026-11-12 the browser reached the dashboard through Dune's own search, which moves between pages without reloading, so the copying script was already in the page and caught all 46 calls from the first. That is 40 widgets, the full set of 18 tiles among them, with data through 2026-11-11 from Dune's run of 10:33 UTC that day. The dashboard keeps only the widgets near the screen loaded, so it was scrolled slowly; fast scrolling skipped the row of 30-day tiles. The raw HAR files are kept outside the repository, in `dune-readings/` next to it. `registry/sources.csv` says a reading made by hand should replace them all.

### Bitwise NEAR ETF (NRR)
- [Bitwise's page](https://nrretf.com) embeds the share count, NEAR in trust, daily NAV and market price, and a list of the trust's wallets. It shows only the current share count and no history. Its dates can differ by section, so each figure is stored with its own date.
- Flow is the change in shares times NAV. SoSoValue's reported flows work out to the same thing: on every day tested, the flow divided by NAV lands on a whole basket of 10,000 shares, the size set in the [prospectus](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002067111/000121390026103106/ea0306486-424b3_bitwise.htm).
- Nearly all of the listed wallets delegate to `bitwise_2.poolv1.near`, and the fund is nearly the whole pool, so one `get_accounts` call at a past block reads its NEAR for that day. Only the listed wallets are stored. Other delegators are people's own wallets, so they are kept as a total.
- The chain settles about one trading day after the trade and nets orders against each other. For example, +60,000 shares on 10-26 and −200,000 on 10-27 appear on chain as −150,000 on 10-28 and +10,000 on 10-29. So `fct_etf_nrr_daily` answers what traded on a day, and `fct_etf_nrr_chain_daily` answers what reached the chain.
- Three checks tie these together. Whole baskets settled on chain add up to the fund's share count once settlement catches up, usually within two trading days (the check allows 5%), chain NEAR is within about 0.2% of the fund's figure, and the seed baskets in the prospectus appear on chain on 2026-09-28. The gap in NEAR may be fees the fund still owes on its rewards; that has not been confirmed.
- History before the first saved snapshot is in `registry/etf_nrr_history.csv`, with a link and a basis on each row: 13 days use a flow reported by SoSoValue, 4 use the gap between two of its cumulative figures, and 7 use baskets settled on chain the next trading day. Those 7, from 10-01 to 10-09, are estimates: the total through 10-12 is $153,000, or 0.2%, below SoSoValue's. From the first snapshot on, flows come directly from Bitwise's share count.
- A missed night loses that day's share count for good, and the next reading's flow covers both days. The chain table is unaffected, because a past block can be read later.
- SoSoValue's own site refuses scripts, so its numbers come by hand from news reports. Those reports contain slips: ChainCatcher printed the 10-23 cumulative figure as $8.82629M and not $88.2629M. A data test holds each reported net-assets figure to shares times NAV.
- The first pool read is stored in the 2026-11-01 snapshot even though it was read on 2026-11-02. It reads only blocks up to the pin, and past blocks do not change. The manifest's `fetched_at` shows the real date.
- From the 2026-11-10 reading on, each one also keeps three figures the page shows for the current day only: the premium or discount to NAV, the days the fund has traded at a premium, at a discount and at NAV, and the 30-day median bid-ask spread. They are in bronze and no model reads them yet. One that is missing is saved as null and reported as a warning, so it cannot cost the night's share count.
- Bitwise's terms limit copying site content, so only figures and the wallet list are stored, never the page. They are used with credit to Bitwise, as decided on 2026-11-13 (`registry/sources.csv`, column `publication_note`).
- Other NEAR products are not tracked: Bitwise's European staking ETP, 21Shares, Virtune, Valour and Grayscale's trust. No daily share count or wallet list was found for them, and their published asset figures were old and did not always agree. One wallet holds 17.9M NEAR in `bitwise_1.poolv1.near` and one holds 10.4M in `svrn_bitwise.poolv1.near`. They could be the European ETP and SVRN, but neither has been confirmed, so neither is counted here.
